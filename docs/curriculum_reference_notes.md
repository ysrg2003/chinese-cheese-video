# Curriculum reference notes

The English curriculum’s early sequence is grounded in two external references consulted on 2026-08-13.

1. The World Xiangqi Federation states that its World Xiangqi Rules were created as a unified standard for Xiangqi competition and provides the English rules document. The curriculum uses WXF as the authoritative reference point for rule-sensitive lessons such as legal movement, direct-facing generals, repetition, and chasing; those lessons are marked as rule-sensitive rather than making unsupported tournament claims.

Source: https://www.wxf-xiangqi.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=269&Itemid=291&lang=en
Rules PDF: https://www.wxf-xiangqi.org/images/wxf-rules/2018_World_XiangQi_Rules_English2018.pdf

2. Chess.com’s beginner explainer confirms the basic teaching sequence used in the curriculum: pieces sit on intersections; the river limits some pieces and changes pawn options; generals and advisors are restricted to palaces; chariots move on open lines; cannons capture by jumping exactly one screen; horses can be blocked at the horse eye; elephants cannot cross the river; and generals may not face directly.

Source: https://www.chess.com/blog/SamCopeland/how-to-play-chinese-chess

These sources support the ordering of foundation, board, piece, rule, and first-game lessons. The curriculum does not treat the Chess.com explainer as the authority for tournament adjudication; WXF remains the required reference for advanced rule lessons.
